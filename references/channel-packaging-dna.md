## Viewer question

Explain one curious everyday food question through the supported tasks and resources of a prehistoric or ancient community.

## Title direction

Name one everyday task, habit or inconvenience and its supported early setting. Use an immediately understandable curious question, localized naturally. Avoid abstract scholarly surveys, fixed headline formulas or unverified assumptions that every ancient group lacked an object or practice.

## Thumbnail direction

People harvesting, selecting, grinding, cooking, preserving and exchanging food; plant comparisons and process diagrams (inside the video only). Original Chibi 2D; actively stage meaningful human action, without a fixed cast ratio. Distinct keyframes for changed states. Favor a Chibi person visibly attempting or solving the specific ordinary-life task in the researched early setting, with readable situational expression and one explanatory object or relationship. No generic shock face, fabricated comic event presented as history or modern-prop anachronism. Objects and diagrams can lead when clearer.

## Role of thumbnail text

Short exact localized number, true claim or everyday question: at most 3 words (Japanese at most 8 characters). Must not repeat any title phrase and must only promise what the video answers. See references/thumbnail-visual-direction.md (Studio rules take precedence).

## Opening promise

Open on a supported food action, ingredient, meal habit, pleasure, need or contrast in a named prehistoric or ancient community; a practical difficulty is optional. Confirm the same supported title/thumbnail promise and deliver useful progress by roughly 0:30.

## Optional title starting points

- How Did [early community] [everyday task]?
- Why Did [ancient community] [specific everyday habit]?
- How Did [ancient community] Deal with [ordinary inconvenience]?

## Thumbnail concepts to choose from

- A Chibi person grinding, cooking or preserving the featured food; show the material change clearly.
- Supported wild and cultivated forms beside a person selecting or harvesting; keep scale truthful.
- People preparing or sharing a meal in a researched setting; make the featured food practice legible.

- Generic shock faces or invented scale/stakes.
