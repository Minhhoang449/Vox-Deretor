# Applied documentary writing framework

Revision: documentary-writing-framework-v1-2026-10-21. User-authorized on 21/10/2026 for new writing and requested revisions across the six active channels. This consolidates the existing topic and editorial workflow; it is not six compulsory chapters or permission to revise approved production.

## The working sequence

Apply [script-packaging-coordination.md](script-packaging-coordination.md) alongside this sequence: the two treatments include provisional title/thumbnail/opening combinations before long narration. Recheck actual full speech and package together before approval; finalize artwork after existing gates.

1. **Earn the question.** State one ordinary viewer question in the audience language and why the answer is worth hearing. Resolve channel lens, place/period/group, exclusions and history through topic-selection.md and scout. Separate curiosity, source feasibility and observed market evidence. A rare object or a popular adjacent video alone does not establish appeal.
2. **Establish the answer.** Before committing the promise, verify its essential answer and causal links through authoritative evidence. Identify the concrete observations/actions that can sustain the middle. Narrow unsupported claims within the agreed subject or research further. Keep a provisional title/thumbnail promise during development; final packaging follows story lock, rather than forcing facts to fit a locked headline.
3. **Compare treatments.** Use the existing topic-selection two-treatment comparison and critical pass; do not create a second parallel approval process. Compare brief openings, first useful answer, distinct passage contributions and earned endings from the same bounded evidence. Select for comprehension, curiosity and channel voice; label same-author review. Candidate treatments are not audience A/B tests or narration approval.
4. **Start with something people do or handle.** Open on a supported action, object, habit, pleasure, need or contrast. Orient the viewer and begin paying the promise early, roughly by 0:30. Do not spend the opening on an introductory historical survey or source administration. Difficulty and humor are optional; no invented personal stakes or universal ancient routine.
5. **Develop understanding.** Each major passage follows an actual question through evidence/action to something newly understood and the next necessary step. The middle must add something beyond the first answer: a material change, meaningful constraint, supported difference or consequence. Answer subquestions as soon as evidence supports them. Cut or merge passages whose deletion loses no distinct understanding; do not confuse another process step with narrative progress. Meaningful changes of place, task, state or explanatory model guide rhythm, not a timer.
6. **Finish and speak naturally.** End when the promise and relevant consequence/limit are paid. No compulsory recap, callback or next-video tease. Run the existing editorial-review-loop on the exact speech: read aloud in the native language, check first-hearing comprehension, pronoun references, terms after their purpose, breath and sentence-rhythm variation. Merge redundant caveats without removing uncertainty that changes the answer. Record actual text/listening/visual review limits and reviewer independence. Present the narration for approval before beats; inspect approved native-speed audio before images.

## Adapted episode spine for every channel

Before drafting continuous narration, fill the **Adapted episode spine** in [the existing story card](templates/story-card.md). Use it to make a reviewable route through the evidence, not to force six equal sections into the script. A compact episode may combine or omit a stage when the viewer question and sources do not need it; change the order when the evidence makes another route clearer.

Use this sequence as a diagnostic:

1. **Recognizable opening and promise:** begin with a supported action, object, habit, need or contrast. State the ordinary-language question that the actual title and thumbnail can truthfully promise.
2. **Early useful answer:** orient the viewer to place, period, people and task, then give one verified piece of progress by about 0:30. Do not make them wait through an intro before learning anything.
3. **Evidence-led chain:** reconstruct the real action, process, rule, finding or adaptation in a clear order. Each major passage must add an observable change, a needed mechanism, a bounded comparison or a consequence.
4. **Consequential middle advance:** show what the opening answer alone cannot explain. Add a supported constraint, coordination, scale change, decision, difference or new evidence. If the middle only repeats the answer in new words, repair or remove it.
5. **Concrete use or implication:** where evidence supports it, connect the process to an object, practice, social effect, place or lived consequence that deepens the same question. This is not a required second case or an invitation to branch into unrelated trivia.
6. **Earned answer and ending:** answer the central question, state the consequence or material limit, and stop. Return to the opening object only if the route has changed what it means; the callback is optional.

Adapt the carrier to the live channel profile rather than borrowing the rope episode's exact sequence: food origins follow a crop or food practice; invention/material episodes follow a task and transformation; everyday-life episodes follow a bounded lived task or routine; social-system episodes follow a convention, classification or institution; Japanese human-history episodes follow their evidence-led human question; Vietnamese geography episodes follow a place-based adaptation, settlement or movement. Use the selected profile's `story_format`, `narration_direction`, `writing_contract` and research-derived visual grammar. A process-step list, chronology, named protagonist, callback, comparison or additional example is included only when it advances that channel's question.

For every major passage, the story card records **active viewer question → source-backed evidence/action/state change → new understanding → next necessary question or payoff**. Apply the deletion test across adjacent passages. The first-30-second progress, consequential middle advance, full answer and actual package alignment are review gates; chapter count, fixed passage lengths, cliffhangers and scheduled re-hooks are not. The spine is an editorial clarity and progression method, not a retention guarantee. After publication, evaluate actual retention and traffic-source context against the exact released script and visuals.

## Selective use of external frameworks

- [yaxeen long-form-youtube](https://github.com/yaxeen/storytelling-skills/blob/main/skills/long-form-youtube/SKILL.md): adapt promise/opening alignment, purposeful progression, varied density and an explicit payoff. Do not import a mandatory loop chain, withheld answer until the end, fixed chapter count, 40–60% re-hook, 20–40-second interrupts, private-worry avatar or compulsory CTA. These are practitioner suggestions, not measured retention guarantees.
- [bmcgauley scriptwriting](https://github.com/bmcgauley/SKILLs/blob/master/scriptwriting/SKILL.md): adapt writing for listening, concrete examples and read-aloud revision. Do not impose a universal sentence word limit, banned-word list, artificial hesitations or English speech habits on Japanese/Vietnamese. No interview/dialogue may be invented to fill a template.
- [Director quality pass](director-quality-pass.md): apply the adopted visual/sound method after narration approval. Lemo's animation and short-film defaults do not govern writing or production.

External files are reference material, not automatically loaded authoring authority. Do not install/invoke an upstream skill as a substitute for this adaptation. Topic research, live channel identity, exact narration approval and documentary boundaries remain in force.

## Review and reuse

Use the existing story-card and research/decision records; do not duplicate state in a new mandatory packet. Record which treatment was chosen and why, first useful answer, what the consequential middle adds, cuts and read-aloud findings. The story /16 and retention /10 reviews still apply to the exact current speech with its fingerprint. A writing pass cannot prove factual completeness, actual listening or audience performance by checking fields.

No change to the 12–20-minute/default-15 target: sustain it with useful supported substance, not padding or stretched audio. Same question does not require the same structure across channels: food practices, material/tool transformations, lived routines, shared conventions, Japanese evidence-led history and Vietnamese geographical adaptation retain their own grammar.

After publication, connect observations to the exact released version and comparable age/traffic context. Unknown demand and unavailable analytics stay unknown. This framework is an editorial method, not a success percentage.
