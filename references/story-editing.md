# Story edit for the selected channel

For new work include everyday-ancient-curiosity.md in criterion 6: quote the ordinary-life question, the bounded early setting and actual approachable, lightly witty speech. A scholarly chronological survey cannot earn full channel fit merely because it is accurate or ancient. Humor has no quota and must not distort facts, cultures or sensitive material. Confirm that none of the three borrowed example subjects was selected.

Review the actual narration independently from the plan. Record channel/topic/slug/version. Quote opening, important middle mechanism/turn and ending; identify the longest static passage and its function.

Apply live question_design: one central question may be carried by a focused task or connected routines in a bounded setting. Do not deduct fit merely because several necessary routines appear or award progression merely because a process has steps. An action, object, habit, pleasure, need or contrast can earn the opening; a difficulty is optional. Check that the spoken middle follows lived actions and distinct understanding rather than repeated source qualifications. Preserve uncertainty that changes the answer. A constructed second-person viewpoint must remain illustrative, without invented private thoughts or a universal day.

Score 0–2 per item:
1. Concrete opening earns the correct central question without misleading suspense.
2. Scope, place/time, stakes and promise are clear.
3. Events, flows, thresholds or choices progress causally in this channel's format.
4. Mechanisms, numbers and comparisons are accurate, plainly explained and bounded.
5. Target-language speech is idiomatic, varied and speakable.
6. The selected channel's narrative grammar and voice are consistently visible in actual passages; explain this evidence.
7. Sources, interpretation, uncertainty, reconstruction and illustrative/hypothetical material are honest.
8. The ending answers the promise and explains relevant consequence/limit without generic recap.

Target 13/16 with criterion 6 at 2/2. No zero for mechanism, spoken naturalness, evidence or payoff. Unsupported essential causality, factual error, fabricated testimony and a broken central promise block approval regardless of totals. A documentary does not need a mission, divergence or invented aftermath. Optional scenarios need explicit assumptions.

Revise highest-impact weaknesses. Report actual version, score, strength, main remaining weakness and whether review is text-only, then run a separate retention pass. Neither score predicts audience performance.

Read the actual speech before consulting old scores. For criterion 3, 2/2 requires distinct, connected advances through the episode; use the adjacent-passage deletion test in scripting-dna.md. Award 1/2 when the mechanism is clear but several passages return to the same conclusion or form an inventory of research. Award 0 when the middle is incoherent or does not develop the question.

For criterion 6, 2/2 requires sourced particulars of this place, event or task to carry the consequential middle and payoff using the selected channel's grammar. Quote those passages and identify their evidence. A local opening and ending surrounding a largely interchangeable general explanation earns at most 1/2; a fundamentally wrong format earns 0. Transferable science remains useful and a real protagonist is not mandatory. The name-substitution test is a diagnostic, not a ban on general explanation.

If the promise contrasts outcomes, identify where the actual sides, relevant conditions and supported difference appear. A regional trend or illustrative scenario cannot prove a missing site-specific outcome. A single-system explanation needs no obligatory second case. Do not recommend approval while an essential promised comparison is absent; research it or propose a narrower promise within the agreed subject. Do not raise scores to reach the target, and do not treat fewer words as proof of stronger storytelling.

Check for production directions or source-administration paragraphs leaking into speech, repeated mechanisms that add no state/change, and comparisons whose promised result never appears. Quote the actual failure and its repair. These are writing defects, not proof that old DNA caused them. Do not claim a higher score until the actual revised version has been reviewed.

## Viewer guidance — no additional score

Read the title/thumbnail promise as a new viewer, then check the first image and actual first 30 seconds of speech. They should confirm the same truthful question and deliver useful, verified progress. Thirty seconds is YouTube's intro analytics point, not a mandatory hook length.

Map major passages as active viewer question → evidence or event change → updated understanding → next question/dependency or payoff. A new passage should either pay a question or move the viewer toward the central answer. Flag a gap that is only repeated, an unrelated branch, or a transition that requires an unsupported causal claim. Check that names, terms and numbers arrive when their purpose is clear and the visual helps explain the same relationship.

Apply the selected channel's actual narrative grammar. The Long Road to Lunch explains food origins and practices through supported selection, preparation and exchange. From Need to Tool connects a practical job to materials, making and adoption. Past the Palace explains a bounded daily task and differences in lived conditions. The Things We Agree On traces the formation, authority and contesting of a social rule or idea. 地球のしくみ帳 connects concrete human-history questions to artifacts and scientific evidence in natural Japanese. Củ Hũ connects conditions of place to supported human adaptation in natural Vietnamese. The psychology layer guides attention; it does not replace the channel DNA, justify invented emotion or require a fixed chapter, cliffhanger or interrupt interval.

If published analytics are available, inspect the corresponding narration and visuals at intro drops, dips, spikes and top moments. Spikes can reflect replay, sharing or confusion. Record what the graph shows separately from the proposed explanation. A text-only pass remains an editorial hypothesis, not measured retention.
