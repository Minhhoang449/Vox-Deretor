# Historical Chibi Visual Direction

This shared prompt look applies to new videos and requested visual revisions across all six active channels. Previously delivered artwork remains as delivered until the user requests new artwork.

## Look and purpose

### Shared prompt core

> Original hand-drawn Chibi 2D educational illustration; friendly expressive compact characters, bold clean dark outlines, warm luminous colors, gentle painterly surface texture, restrained cel shading, clear silhouettes and a strong focal action. Keep objects, settings, maps and diagrams in the same illustrated world. Adapt scene, palette and period details to the researched episode; do not reproduce the reference image's subject or composition.

Use one shared prompt look for every new video across all six active channels: original hand-drawn Chibi 2D educational illustration, friendly expressive compact characters when people help explain the passage, bold clean dark outlines, warm luminous colors, gentle painterly surface texture, restrained cel shading, and a clear focal action. Give settings and objects enough illustrated detail to feel like the same world while keeping silhouettes, staging and explanatory relationships easy to read on a phone. Apply this finish consistently to people, artifacts, buildings, landscapes, maps, cutaways and diagrams. Adapt palette, clothing, materials, architecture, geography and object design to the researched subject and place. The attached Amarna water-jar image is a style reference only: do not repeat its person, pose, clothing, wall, jar, layout or Egyptian palette when they do not fit the episode. Do not imitate a named creator, publisher, character, or thumbnail design.

The shared look is not a fixed scene template. The viewer question and evidence decide what appears in each frame; use characters only when meaningful human action clarifies the explanation. Keep facial expressions warm and legible, not caricatured. Maintain coherent character identity within an episode when references exist.

Chibi is an illustration language, not evidence. Research notes and asset records carry provenance. Keep important factual distinctions explicit: sourced artifact versus reconstruction, documented action versus illustrative staging, and known fact versus inference.

## Channel-specific guardrails

Use each live profile for food processes, invention/craft, past daily life, social rules, Japanese prehistory or human geography. Actively stage supported human actions and experiences with period/place-specific clothing and tools. Do not carry over the retired battle-only or medieval-only topic limits. No fixed people ratio, mandatory presenter or invented testimony; maps, objects and diagrams lead when clearer.

## 地球のしくみ帳 explanatory staging — 20/10/2026

Use clear outlines, uncluttered backgrounds and a limited palette for original chibi frames. Choose the concrete object, supported task, map or comparison that explains the current narration. Quantities can use readable, accurately scaled graphics and brief Japanese labels; illustrative icons are not measured data. Characters and diagrams have different jobs, with no fixed ratio. A new composition adds a meaningful state, scale, task or fact. Learn explanatory clarity from the inspected Ink Explainer reference without copying stick figures, layouts or scene sequence. Camera movement cannot supply body movement or a missing physical event.

## Frame workflow and limits

Use Codex image generation after approved narration and narration-audio review. Generate distinct keyframes for changed actions or facts. The still renderer may add camera movement, but it does not animate body movement, expressions, object use or character-to-character action. Freeze the key factual action in each frame; do not imply physical animation through camera movement alone. A true character-animation backend would be a separate production change.

Inside the video, use maps, timelines, artifacts and cross-sections when they explain the narration better than a character scene; reserve diagrams and arrows for the one core mechanism. Never put a diagram or arrow on a thumbnail.

## Studio rules — 22/10/2026

- All channels use Chibi 2D; channels differ by palette, setting and thumbnail layout.
- Thumbnails centre one emotional Chibi character; see [thumbnail visual direction](thumbnail-visual-direction.md).
- Draw only sourced details. **Damaged, missing or interpreted parts of an artifact must be shown as such** (broken edge, faded or dashed reconstruction), never completed as if intact.

## Past mistakes — do not repeat

1. Stone pulling: crowds hauling blocks drawn into scenes whose source says nothing about it.
2. Nemes headdress on labourers: the royal striped headcloth put on workers or villagers.
3. A jar stand that looks like a well: a round stone support beside "no well" text reads as a well.
4. A donkey that is only a hypothesis: drawing an animal the source only says "may have" been used.
- Museum photos or references need a `licence` field (Open Access checked) before use.

 Localized text is optional and must be human-checked for spelling, historical accuracy, phone readability and crop.

## Tone — every image (user decision 22/10/2026)

Every image, thumbnail and video still, is cute, humorous and slightly mischievous Chibi: exaggerated, readable expressions (cheeky grin, comic strain, wide-eyed surprise) and playful body language. The humour comes from the character's reaction to a real, sourced situation. Never mock real people, ethnic groups or cultures, and never add a detail the sources do not support just to make a joke.
