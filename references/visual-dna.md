## Explanatory stills — 17/10/2026

Plan framing around what the viewer needs to understand: orientation, hidden relationship, supported action/state or consequence. The order varies with the passage. Pan/zoom directs attention; a changed fact or action needs an appropriate original state image under image-reuse.md. More motion or more stills does not establish a better story; inspect whether the sequence actually communicates the change.

## Viewer orientation — 17/10/2026

The opening still should make the same documented question or state visible as the title, thumbnail and first spoken line. During explanation, use maps, cross-sections, comparisons and labels to clarify the current relationship. Let meaningful changes in time, place, task, evidence or physical state guide image transitions; keep a stable diagram while the viewer needs to understand the same relation. A transition can orient attention but must not imply a new event, causality or motion absent from the record. Avoid arbitrary visual interrupts, duplicate text and camera movement added only to manufacture activity. See viewer-guidance.md for the cognitive research and limits.

## Tone — every image (user decision 22/10/2026)

Every image, thumbnail and video still, is cute, humorous and slightly mischievous Chibi: exaggerated, readable expressions (cheeky grin, comic strain, wide-eyed surprise) and playful body language. The humour comes from the character's reaction to a real, sourced situation. Never mock real people, ethnic groups or cultures, and never add a detail the sources do not support just to make a joke.
