# Documentary reconstruction, illustration and optional fiction

Follow the active [channel documentary DNA](scripting-dna.md). Documented events and real mechanisms are the default. A fictional person, invented exchange, intervention or traveler is never required. Keep research classifications in the ledger; state material uncertainty naturally where it affects a claim.

## Distinguish the layers

- **Sourced fact:** actual historical/scientific conditions, observations, recorded actions and quotations. Verify sources, scope and material limits.
- **Interpretation:** an explanation supported by evidence but open to alternatives. Attribute or qualify when material.
- **Scenario assumption:** a granted input, changed decision, resource budget, technology or cooperation rule. Explain consequential grants before using them.
- **Conditional inference:** what could follow from those inputs and mechanisms. State dependencies; it is neither a real event nor a certain forecast.
- **Reconstruction:** show supported actions or spatial relationships without claiming the generated scene was recorded. Do not invent real-person dialogue, private thoughts or testimony.
- **Illustrative example:** explicitly introduce representative or fictional people/examples. They can explain a verified rule, but cannot establish that rule or substitute for a real person's recorded experience.
- **Fictional drama:** optional only within a clearly framed fictional or specifically selected hypothetical episode; never a default documentary device.

An invented conversation cannot establish a real law, price, motive or ecological mechanism. Do not invent sources, statistics, archival testimony or quotations attributed to real people. Keep actual quotations traceable. Imagined choices by historical actors can appear as explicitly hypothetical scenario decisions, without claiming to know their real private thoughts or giving them invented quotations as testimony.

## Counterfactual divergence

A mission may intentionally change a recorded event. First establish what actually happened and where the scenario diverges. Within the hypothetical branch, outcomes may differ; keep that branch distinct from the factual baseline in speech, the ledger and visuals. Reorient when returning to actual history. Do not impose the old rule that fictional intervention can never change history; do not silently overwrite documented history either.

## Spoken frame and review

For an explicitly selected hypothetical episode, introduce its premise at the relevant hinge. Repeat a qualification only when changing branches, scales or consequential assumptions would otherwise mislead. Keep full classifications and source IDs in notes. Documentary narration follows the selected channel's question, evidence-led progression, answer and relevant consequences; it needs no intervention verdict or fictional future.

Review the actual opening, essential mechanism or turn, and ending for clear evidence boundaries. Empirical mechanisms need sources; explicitly hypothetical outcomes need a visible causal path and uncertainty. Keep harm respectful. Describe a film as hypothetical only when that is its selected format, and identify substantial invented dramatic scenes. Preserve approved legacy media at their recorded versions.
