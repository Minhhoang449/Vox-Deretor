## One development loop

Use the existing shortlist, story card and `marketing-research.md`; do not create a second approval packet. The coordinator owns consistency across the following stages. These are responsibilities, not a requirement to spawn agents.

1. **Resolve scope and history.** Read live channel, language/market, topic exclusions and scout. State the audience-language everyday question and why its answer matters. Follow topic-selection.md, including separate finalist research and two distinct treatments of the leading viable subject.
2. **Develop the package before long narration.** Once essential evidence is available, compare two distinct provisional title/thumbnail/opening combinations alongside those treatments. Each records: recognizable subject phrase; audience question; supported answer; title; thumbnail focal object/action/relationship and optional exact text; short spoken opening; useful progress by roughly 0:30; final payoff. These are text concepts, not generated assets or a locked headline. Let evidence change the package before drafting.
3. **Check language and discovery intent.** Research the topic in the configured audience language/region. Record dated queries and result URLs separately for each finalist. Inspect whether a subject phrase refers to the intended history, a recipe, entertainment or another meaning. If autocomplete is actually available, record query, locale, date and suggestions; otherwise mark it unobserved. Resolve ambiguity with a natural qualifier and inspect topic-specific results. Suggestions show that people type a phrase, not volume. The title's lead phrase must pass `scripts/research_autocomplete.py`; an unsuggested proper name (site, artifact) moves to the description and tags, not the title lead. A network error blocks, never guesses.
4. **Choose the treatment and write continuous speech.** The critical pass challenges factual support, audience clarity, channel voice, title/thumbnail honesty and runtime feasibility. Record why the chosen combination beats the alternative editorially. Map each major passage to its question, supported action/evidence, distinct new understanding and next dependency. Pay useful partial answers promptly; never hold everything for the ending. Keep the consequential middle grounded in this place, period and community. Plan 8–16 minutes with the configured voice budget.
5. **Review the actual script and package together.** Quote actual opening, consequential middle and ending. Check that the title's subject and question are identifiable early, the thumbnail relationship is explained, and the ending gives the supported answer or precise limit. Trace each package claim to an actual passage and its sources. If one fails, revise the package or research/revise speech; do not invent a scene to rescue a headline. Apply editorial-review-loop.md, then separate story /16 and retention /10 reviews on the exact speech fingerprint. Label same-author, text-only and unavailable-analytics limits. Present the narration and provisional package together for narration approval; the package is editorial context, not approval of unmade artwork.
6. **Finalize after story lock.** Recheck market observations and finalize 1–3 substantive package candidates from the locked story. Follow the existing approval, native audio review and image gates before artwork. Generate thumbnails in the live Chibi 2D style, check actual phone-size images and exact localized text, then use the existing exporter schema. Measured chapters and final metadata follow the finished timeline. No new required keys in beats.json.
7. **Learn from publication.** Record real results separately from topic decisions and editorial hypotheses. Compare available traffic-source CTR, viewing and retention against the exact released package and speech. Missing Studio access remains unavailable. Neither this loop nor a review score predicts performance.

### Working spine and draft handoff

Use the proposed eight beats as treatment options: familiar entry; bounded scene/artifact; clear question; supported unexpected detail; useful partial answer; consequential evidence chain; earned payoff; optional changed-meaning return. Prefer an immediately legible action/object in the first roughly ten seconds, question and orientation by roughly thirty, and a relevant supported detail in the first minute when the evidence allows. These are drafting targets, not measured timestamps, a compulsory named protagonist or eight chapters. Review the actual native audio later. Required: state the main question and one hanging question within the first 60 seconds, answer the hanging question near the end, and close by returning to the opening object or scene once, with its changed meaning. Do not append two callback endings.

Each draft handoff includes the full continuous narration, chosen title and one-line thumbnail concept, links to the alternate treatment and native-market keyword/intent ledger, and concise passage-level visual feasibility notes: what could be shown / exact factual source locator / observation versus interpretation or illustration. Also mark 2-3 visual humor beats in the notes (never in narration): passage, the supported fact it rides on, and one small playful chibi action (exaggerated expression or a mischievous side detail) that does not alter artifacts, clothing or tools. Prefer the hook, the middle turn and the payoff. This is research planning only; detailed shots, image prompts and generated images remain after narration approval. Source locators belong in notes. An episode's current visual style and public-description rules retain authority.

Run `python scripts/draft_review_checks.py <narration.md>`; optionally pass `--evidence <claim-mappings.json>` with a list of objects containing one-based `line`, exact `quote`, `source_locator` and `support`. Inspect the JSON diagnostics and manually review semantic repetition, factual entailment and source coverage. The command detects exact repeated paragraphs and stale/missing mappings only; it cannot verify a source, find all paraphrases or approve a draft. Record every actual blocker with the speech line/excerpt, reason, supporting source and precise repair. Missing evidence or a broken promise blocks editorial readiness; purely stylistic suggestions do not become factual failures.

### Six manual questions before presenting speech

1. Does the opening's action/analogy make a true claim and begin answering the actual title question? A familiar flexible object does not prove the same construction method.
2. What distinct understanding does each passage add? Merge repeated explanations and immediate summaries that add nothing. Each idea, number or example appears at most twice (first use and payoff). No one-sentence paragraphs.
3. Can each factual scene role, posture, tool, count, date and scientific mechanism be traced to inspected evidence? On revision, record changed details and recheck their sources. An unsourced scientific claim needs research, removal or a genuinely supported narrower statement, not a vague generalization.
4. Is the wording no stronger than its source? Keep interpretation qualified where it matters; an earlier attribution never turns a later inference into established observation. No standalone caveat sentence before 2:00 (a single precision word inside the claim is allowed and required when the source is uncertain; never state more than the source); say each caveat once, where it matters, and keep the total to 3-4 sentences.
5. Do the proposed thumbnail and any Short match the supported roles, materials and answer? Reassess artwork when the represented fact changes; regenerate when it becomes inaccurate. Studio rules in references/thumbnail-visual-direction.md are mandatory for every channel: one emotional chibi character in the centre, no diagrams or arrows, at most 3 words of text (Japanese at most 8 characters).
6. Does the ending pay the promise once, and does the whole draft sound natural in this channel's language? Record exact passage evidence and actual listening limits, then use the existing separate story/retention reviews.

### SEO, Shorts and publication follow-up

Inspect a manageable native-market comparison set, aiming for about fifteen relevant videos when available; record actual coverage instead of filling a quota with unrelated results. Treat familiar everyday questions as editorial leads, but a technical process can serve them when it advances the channel's bounded question. Public view/subscriber ratios remain optional descriptive snapshots, not thresholds for copying titles or rejecting topics.

Use a recognizable concrete subject phrase and a truthful curiosity angle. Check spelling and ambiguity through actual market observations; the title's lead phrase must pass `scripts/research_autocomplete.py` (observed suggestion in the audience locale, inside the first 40 characters, JA 20); autocomplete shows that people type the phrase, not volume. Put the useful subject naturally in the description opening. Studio tags remain a small relevant supporting list, without a fixed 5–8 quota. Retain internal sources and required attribution; do not add a public bibliography by default.

Shorts (Studio rule, approved by the channel owner): every long video proposes 2 Shorts by default, 25–40 seconds each, cut from named narration passages. Each Short opens with a question about something the viewer already knows, gives its own true self-contained answer, and contains no hedging. Fact-check the hook against the dossier. The last line must point to the long video, and the export records the long video as the Related video. A Short must not answer the long video's open question, so viewers still have a reason to click through. Do not depict claims the sources only call possible (for example a donkey carrier). Publishing schedules and cross-platform posts are prepared locally only and need the owner's approval each time. Short titles pass the same title gate as long videos (`scripts/research_autocomplete.py`: searched phrase first, max 60 characters, Japanese 32) and must not duplicate the long video's title, so the two do not compete for one search phrase. The Short's hook must be quoted verbatim from the long video's narration (the export checks this). Default posting plan, pending owner approval: Short 1 one day after the long video, Short 2 three days after. End screens can only link to this channel's own videos and Shorts, never to other channels, so competitor adjacency comes from title, tags and description only.

Use 48-hour and seven-day post-publication checkpoints when actual data is supplied or a follow-up is requested. Record impressions, traffic-source CTR and thirty-second retention with the exact package/speech versions and window. Clearly readable Studio screenshots can be transcribed with provenance; missing fields remain unavailable. Low CTR or intro retention triggers diagnosis against comparable traffic and samples, not an automatic 2%/50% rewrite. Separate packaging mismatch from weak opening evidence; a revision to published narration requires its own production decision and approval. No automation or account change is created by this guidance.

Open concretely with supported action, an object, habit, pleasure, need or contrast. A bounded community can carry a story when no individual is known. A real named person is useful when the evidence supports their role, never compulsory. Do not turn an anonymous archaeological find into a known cook's biography. Familiarity is an entry point, not a requirement for a modern second-person hook. Prefer an early relevant surprising detail when supported; do not manufacture disbelief or require one on a timer. Give the central question early enough to orient the opening; avoid a minute of setup before useful explanation. Retain uncertainty at the claim it changes, even in the first minute. End with the answer and its relevant consequence or limit, without stacked conclusions or a mandatory callback.

## Evaluation of the supplied comparison

Studio rules (user-approved 21–22/10/2026) take precedence over older guidance in this repository. Decisions on the pasted comparison:

Adopt: coherent question/title/thumbnail/opening/payoff; recognizable subject wording; explicit earned answer; distinctive channel voice; purposeful concrete details; mobile image review; tags as supporting work; a familiar everyday opening; one hanging question before 0:60 answered at the end; return to the opening object; autocomplete gate on the title lead; one centred Chibi character on thumbnails; 2 Shorts per long video, each ending with a pointer to it.

Soft signals, not hard gates: views/sub >= 0.8 and views/day flag possible breakout topics (ignore ratios for channels under ~100 subs); the 4/5 topic score ranks candidates. CTR and 0:30 retention are judged against the channel's own average until ~5 tracked videos give a measured figure.

Not adopted: mandatory myths, named protagonists or danger; guaranteed superiority of sparse posting; withholding every answer until the end (give the first useful answer early, keep one question open); a separate art style per channel (Chibi 2D for all).

Official guidance checked 21/10/2026: YouTube supports both [searchable and intriguing titles](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12340300), accurate concise wording, simple readable images and audience-specific evaluation. Its [tags guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402) gives tags a minimal discovery role except useful spelling variants. This supports prioritizing the story and package; it does not establish the supplied document's causal explanations of particular videos. The CTR FAQ fetch was blocked; no new claim relies on that fetch.
