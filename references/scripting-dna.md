Question-design revision: everyday-life-question-v2-2026-10-21. Open with an action, object, habit, pleasure, need or contrast; a difficulty is optional. A coherent set of daily routines is not automatically a chronological lecture, and one physical process is not automatically progressive. Keep the central question, setting and social variation clear; each passage adds distinct understanding. Source-backed lived actions lead the speech, while source administration and author instructions stay in notes. Compress repeated qualifications with the same function without deleting material uncertainty. Second person may invite an explicitly illustrative viewpoint; it does not require a mission and cannot prove private thoughts, an individual experience or a universal routine. Use [topic-selection.md](topic-selection.md) before selecting a question.
Open on something concrete that earns a single accurate question. The title, thumbnail, first image and first spoken line must point to the same truthful promise. Deliver useful progress early. Orient geography, time and scale. Let scenes, causal diagrams, comparisons or process states carry the explanation; no lecture waiting behind decorative illustrations. Reveal a mechanism where it explains a change. Close with the supported answer and consequence; a familiar object, place or interaction may return when its meaning has changed.

Narration defaults to warm conversational curiosity and light situational wit through familiar comparisons, concrete practical difficulties and supported unexpected details. Humor density follows the material; never force a joke into suffering or invent dialogue, thoughts, events or ancient incompetence. Keep explanation approachable rather than lecture-like. No fixed chapter count, joke cadence, protagonist, countdown or mandatory future horizon.

## Keep the writing process outside the story

Source locators, review labels, instructions to an editor and shot/prompt directions belong in notes, not narration. Explain the event or process itself instead of telling a producer how it should be reconstructed. State a material date, uncertainty or illustrative assumption naturally at the claim that needs it; do not remove necessary qualifications to make speech shorter.

Do not keep a passage merely because it lists a mechanism accurately. Each passage must add a useful state, causal step, comparison, response or consequence. If the same explanation returns, it must answer a new dependency. A promise of two different experiences needs the actual bounded difference before the ending. Pay the event, place or interaction's central promise; do not end with instructions to research it later.

When a stored premise has another DNA revision, editorial revision or channel format, use it as production history only. The live context carries forward period/place, not its opening, plot, granted resources, answer or imagined consequences. Rewrites require fresh research and narration approval; compatible current drafts and explicit episode exceptions remain available.

## Evidence and representation
Important claims require independently checked primary or authoritative evidence. Public YouTube performance is discovery only. Keep observation, interpretation, reconstruction, illustrative example and hypothetical scenario distinct. A source-based reconstruction may show supported movement or mechanism; it cannot invent dialogue, private thoughts or testimony for real people. Date jurisdiction-specific financial or institutional rules and avoid universal thresholds.

A what-if is available only when selected for the episode. Identify the real baseline, divergence, consequential assumptions and inference limits. It is not the default plot and does not replace a documented explanation.

## Story execution — 17/10/2026

Execution revision: documentary-story-execution-v4. This applies evidence-informed viewer guidance to new drafts and requested revisions; it does not reapprove or rewrite recorded production. Read [viewer guidance](viewer-guidance.md) for the research basis and limits.

### Choose the promise from evidence

Before drafting, state the answer the episode can actually demonstrate and the observations, operating records, findings or bounded results that support its essential links. Choose the narrative carrier from this material. A broad question cannot be fulfilled merely by accurately explaining the general science. Research a missing essential link or narrow the promise within the agreed subject; do not silently change a bound episode's identity or invent a case to complete the outline.

When the promise asks why two places, periods or groups have different outcomes, identify the actual named or clearly bounded sides and what can be compared. Check relevant dates, definitions, measurements and conditions; explain material differences before drawing a causal or numerical contrast. A regional trend cannot substitute for an unobserved effect at one structure, and a labeled illustrative example cannot establish an empirical result. A question about how one system works does not require a second case.

### Make the mechanism belong to this episode

Use general science and institutional explanation where needed, then connect the essential relationship to a sourced condition, action, finding or result at this place, event or service task. Those particulars must carry the consequential middle and payoff, not appear only in the opening and final paragraph. A process can supply the story without a protagonist or invented crisis.

As a diagnostic, replace the place, company or battle name in the middle: if the explanation and outcome need almost no change, inspect whether the episode has a generic spine. This does not prohibit transferable science, require a named person, or demand field measurements in every paragraph. The question is what the particular episode contributes beyond its label. Do not add unsourced human stakes or force two locations into every story.

### Progression, explanation and transitions

For each meaningful passage, identify what the viewer can now explain, distinguish or predict that was unavailable before it, and the evidence or explanatory step that supplies that advance. A changed physical state, new finding, demonstrated constraint or comparison can supply progress. A new study name, term, qualification or restatement of the same conclusion cannot do so by itself. Apply a deletion test across adjacent passages: if removing one loses no distinct understanding or necessary scope, merge or cut it. Keep useful orientation, material qualifications and brief reminders when context makes them necessary; no fixed passage length or reveal interval applies.

Establish the job or constraint before technical names. Use a familiar comparison for the relevant relationship only; do not turn an analogy into an unsupported physical claim. Keep numbers that explain a meaningful scale, threshold or difference. Drop an unhelpful data comparison instead of reading several values and then explaining why they cannot be compared.

Distinguish a sourced causal consequence, an explanatory prerequisite and the next task of the same system. Only the first permits claiming that one solution caused the next problem. Do not invent surprise, discovery order, private thoughts, a unique solution or a crisis to connect sections. The main answer can arrive early or late; each intervening passage pays a useful question. Later branches serve the title/opening promise or a demonstrated consequence. No fixed cliffhanger interval applies.

### Guide attention with evidence-based curiosity

Treat curiosity as a clear, answerable gap in the viewer's understanding, not an excuse to hide a known fact. Confirm the title/thumbnail promise in the first image and first spoken line. Use the first 30 seconds as a practical check: has the viewer received orientation and a useful piece of verified progress? This matches YouTube's intro measurement window; it is not a universal hook length or a guarantee of retention.

Map the central question and answerable subquestions before drafting. For each major passage, record the viewer's active question, the evidence or event change, what the viewer can now understand, and the next question or dependency that follows. Pay smaller questions as soon as evidence allows. A question can remain open while the episode genuinely advances it; do not repeat teasers or invent a suspense beat. The one Studio hanging question set in the first minute is answered near the end.

Use meaningful changes of time, place, task, physical state or explanatory model to orient the viewer and form natural passage boundaries. Explain the practical job before technical terms, introduce one new relation at a time where possible, and keep numbers that help the viewer judge scale, threshold or outcome. The matching still, map or diagram should clarify the same relation. Use a new image when a sourced state, task, scale or model changes; pan/zoom, captions and pattern interrupts are not substitutes for explanation. Human relevance comes from documented consequences, not an invented private worry.

Review the middle for an actual change in understanding, not a scheduled percentage-based re-hook. After publication, treat the 30-second intro, dips, spikes and top moments as observations to investigate against the exact words and visuals. A spike may mean interest, sharing or confusion. Compare similar videos and traffic sources where possible; never present self-scores as audience behavior.

### Evidence in speech and a single ending

Keep access logs, abstract-only reading status, tool limitations and instructions to the author in the dossier/review. Speak the uncertainty, date, jurisdiction, scope or assumption that changes the audience's interpretation at the affected claim. An investigative method belongs in narration when its finding changes the causal model. If an essential claim is unsupported, narrow it or research further; a disclaimer does not validate it.

Close once the supported answer and relevant consequence or unresolved limit are clear. End by returning to the opening object with its changed meaning (required, once); no route recap, universal lesson or future/climate section. An optional discussion question comes after the payoff. Do not stack several conclusions.
