# Comparing channel DNA with observed reference videos

Use this after topic research and before approval of new narration. It complements story /16 and retention /10; it never predicts audience retention or proves superiority to a publisher.

## Evidence first

For each comparison record video URL, channel identity, observation date, selection basis, duration, title, thumbnail and inspected spans. Classify evidence as metadata, chapters, sampled playback, original-language transcript, translated transcript, or whole-video review. A translated/automatic transcript can support a structural comparison, but cannot establish original phrasing, voice quality or exact numbers. Check factual claims independently. Separate old snapshot views from current public counts. Popular in a screenshot is not necessarily the all-time channel leader.

Local-language discovery must fit the actual topic. History channels do not validate Japanese/Vietnamese geography demand merely because the language matches. Sparse query results mean that query yielded weak evidence, not that the market lacks demand. Reject unrelated shorts, national-pride lists and wrong-language results from the direct comparison set. Record any remaining market gap.

## Make the comparison concrete

Choose a comparable narrative task: opening question, one mechanism sequence or an ending, with a similar information load and passage length. Note when topics, format or duration differ. Compare what the actual text does, not intentions in a DNA document.

1. Opening specificity and accurate promise.
2. Time/place/scale and first useful answer.
3. Causal progression and changes in understanding.
4. Mechanism clarity, visual explainability and information load.
5. Human stakes, language naturalness and appropriate tone.
6. Evidence, scope, uncertainty and earned payoff.

Quote the local version's opening, essential middle and ending. Identify its longest explanation and what it changes. Record a first draft, diagnosed issue, revised text and why the change helps. Run story and retention as separate editorial passes; if the same author performs both, label them self-reviews. Do not award a full point just because a field or keyword exists. Missing essential causality or fabricated testimony blocks approval regardless of totals.

Use the live writing_contract for each format. Earlier benchmark videos do not restore retired channel topics.

## What the result means

Keep DNA readiness, script quality, image/prompt quality, synthesized-audio review and finished-video performance as separate statuses. A banner is not an episode thumbnail. A short benchmark is not an approved 8–16 minute episode. No completed new episode means full-video quality remains unassessed.

Conclude with specific dimensions improved, remaining limitations and next measurable question. To claim better audience performance requires comparable published analytics, traffic context and a sufficient sample; public views and self-assigned scores are insufficient.
